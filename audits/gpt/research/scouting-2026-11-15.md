## Evidence and transfer limits

Novy-Marx and Velikov distinguish narrower entry from wider retention. Section 5.1 gives the 10%/20% example, and Table 6 reports turnover savings alongside some gross-return sacrifice for their US long–short anomaly portfolios. Those portfolios use NYSE breakpoints and different frequencies and weighting. The author PDF grounds the rule. The SSRN record lists a February 2026 revision whose text was not verified. [Author manuscript](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf) · [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv063) · [Revision record](https://ssrn.com/abstract=2535173)

Chen and Velikov's FEDS Appendix A.4 tunes exits in-sample and finds little benefit among lower-turnover groups. Outcomes depend on weighting. Their separate 2023 published abstract reports small aggregate post-publication net returns across 204 anomalies. Neither version tests this project’s long-only, daily, participation-capped rule. The strongest alternative is that stale holdings dilute gross signal and alter exposure or liquidity enough to outweigh spread savings. [FEDS manuscript](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2020039pap.pdf) · [Published version](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022109022000874)

## Preregistered synthetic mechanics check

Use ten synthetic instruments, one common point-in-time universe, fixed externally supplied scores and no real data. Rank descending using tied average rank divided by eligible count. Enter at percentile at most 0.10. Retain only a positive realized position when eligible and at most 0.20. Outside the eligible set, the exit request remains subject to the normal fill rules. At fewer than ten eligible names, take no new positions. Use unchanged configured leverage and next-open execution. With no eligible held or entry names, schedule zero target exposure. Target fixed 10% of open equity per eligible portfolio name and leave remaining capital in cash, with no borrowing or shorting. The first acceptance criterion checks target membership; the account comparison separately reports resizing turnover and exposure.

- Warm up the liquidity/volatility windows with valid synthetic bars, using ADV and volatility windows two and a four-session rebalance stride. Use explicit decisions at close t0 and t4, with fills at opens t1 and t5. Both histories target A at rank 1 of 10 at t0. At t1, A gets no fill in one history because its bar is missing, and a positive clipped fill in the other. Supply valid A bars at t2–t4 to re-establish both trailing estimates. At t4, use identical eligible scores with B rank 1 and A rank 2.
- Expected t4 target membership for the 10/20 rule is **{B} after rejection and {A, B} after clipping**. A target-memory control gives {A, B} in both. A 10/10 comparator gives {B} in both. Require identical membership for both repeated executions, no trading before the next open, and exact share/cash/fees reconciliation against an independently written decimal ledger on constant-price, explicit-spread controls. A future-only price/volume perturbation must preserve earlier decisions. A no-signal cash control should have no transactions or costs. No success can be claimed if the warmed fixture has different eligible names or the histories fail to produce zero versus positive shares.
- Budget is one prespecified buffered variant and one primary unbuffered comparator, with the two execution histories and named controls. No tuning, and no trial attempted. If mechanics pass later, preregister a separate exploratory US equity study before reading any data. Use the same frozen five-day reversal signal, eligibility, daily close decisions and next-open fills on chronological pre-2025, already-inspected authorized data. Require licensed point-in-time membership, delisting settlements, and lagged dollar volume with executable cost inputs. Exclude reserved windows. No borrow charges or funding leverage for the long-only book. Include identical commission, spread, square-root impact and participation cap, plus zero/doubled-cost and two-session-delay sensitivities. Report paired incremental net returns with dependence-aware intervals, traded notional, exposure, drawdown and cap-related rejects. Reject the proposed benefit if net improvement does not survive realistic costs and delay or merely reflects altered gross exposure. A lower turnover number alone is insufficient.

**Feasibility.** The engine updates actual shares after rejection or clipping and provides only eligible scores to the strategy callback. The CLI uses the default strategy. The Python API can inject a custom strategy and emit predetermined weights, which can validate execution and accounting. It cannot implement a single-pass fill-aware buffer through the current score-only callback. The cheapest adequate alternative today is the existing strategy plus cost/delay stress and a separate precomputed-target accounting oracle. Product changes are outside this audit. Engineering effort is estimated at one small research-oracle implementation after isolation, with a separate design decision for a reusable execution-state interface. ## Deterministic exploration draw

Small-sample statistical calibration. Kiefer and Vogelsang's sections 4.2 and 5 support matching nonstandard critical values to the fixed bandwidth fraction and kernel, with a size/power tradeoff. The project instead uses normal-tail p-values after Bartlett HAC and applies Holm to confirmation p-values. Keep this as a method lead attached to **IS-2026-10-24-1**. No new idea ID or defect is established. A future synthetic comparison should prespecify dependence, overlapping horizons, missing sessions, empirical null size, power and familywise error. Hold the selected family and trial count fixed. The evidence does not justify changing only the bandwidth or applying fixed-b quantiles from a different kernel.
